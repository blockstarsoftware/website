The problem

For Apple Music listeners with great hi-fi gear

You own your music: high-resolution files, 96 kHz, 192 kHz. You play them in Apple Music: your library, your playlists, one app. And you have great headphones or speakers, a great DAC and amp, a system built to deliver all the detail the recording has.

There is only one problem. Apple Music does not offer bit-perfect playback of local high-resolution files. macOS runs your sound output at one standard setting, often 44.1 or 48 kHz at 16-bit, and that setting does not change when you play local music in Apple Music. macOS has no way to automatically switch to the higher rate and bit depth a track needs. So your 96 kHz and 192 kHz files, most of them 24-bit, are converted down to 16-bit and the standard rate before they reach your DAC, and the detail in your files never makes it there.

Fortunately, there is BitPolish. You keep using Apple Music on your local files, and the music reaches your hardware the way the file was made, at its sample rate and its bit depth.

How it works

What exactly does BitPolish do?

1

Switch on Bit-Perfect Listening

Turn your DAC or amp down first. Then set the Mac and Music to 100%, and set your listening level on the DAC.

2

Press play in Music

3

BitPolish gets to work

It has two modes.

Automatic

BitPolish changes your DAC's sample rate on the fly. As each track starts, it sets the DAC to that track's rate, whatever the file is and your DAC supports, for Apple Lossless, AAC and MP3, and locks the bit depth at 24-bit by default, so 24-bit tracks stay 24-bit. A DAC that goes higher can be set to its maximum.

Manual

Changing sample rate takes the DAC a moment, about a second on some hardware, so in automatic mode a track at a new rate begins after a short gap. If you know what plays next, Manually Prime DAC removes it. It is a row of rate buttons in BitPolish's panel: pause Music, click the rate of the track you are about to play (Get Info in Music shows each file's rate), then press play. The DAC switches while nothing is playing, so the track starts clean. You never have to use it. It is there for the tracks where the gap would bother you.

Details

And the details that make it trustworthy

Tells you the truth

An asterisk beside the rate in the menu bar means playback is not bit-perfect right now, because the volume is below 100% or your DAC refuses the rate. Rates your DAC will not take are faded.

Restores everything

Before it touches your DAC, BitPolish saves its format. Switch off, quit, relaunch after a crash or replug the DAC, and it goes back exactly as it was. BitPolish never changes volume.

Never surprises your speakers

If BitPolish moved your sound to the DAC, it stays there until you click Return, so nothing suddenly plays out loud.

Leave it on, or not

Open at login, keep Bit-Perfect Listening on and Show in Dock are one click away. By default it does none of them.

Stays current

It checks for updates once a day while it is running, and nothing runs when it is not.

Philosophy

Built for listening sessions

You sit down with your music, switch BitPolish on, listen, and switch it off when you are done. Everything in the app follows from that.

While it is on, it follows the music. Pause, and the DAC holds the last track's rate, so pressing play picks up right where you left off. When you switch it off, BitPolish steps aside and your DAC goes back to how it was.

Asks for nothing.

No permission prompts of any kind: no AppleScript, Accessibility, audio recording or Media Library access. It reads Music's own log, its track-change notifications and the system's open-file table.

Hands off when off.

While it is off, it reads and changes nothing.

Small on purpose.

No Dock icon and no login item unless you choose them. Quit it and nothing stays behind.

External DACs only.

Built-in output, Bluetooth, AirPlay and virtual devices are refused, because they cannot be bit-perfect.

Questions

Requirements and limits

Which Macs does BitPolish run on?

A Mac with Apple Silicon and macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Does my adapter or cable matter?

A track arrives bit-perfect only if every link can carry it: the Mac, any adapter, the cable and the DAC's input.

Does it work with other music players?

Apple Music only. Audirvana and Roon are great players that manage the DAC themselves. If you are happy in one of them, you do not need BitPolish.

Do I need an administrator account?

An administrator account, to follow rates. On a standard account the bit-depth lock still works, and BitPolish says so.

Why is there a short gap before some tracks?

In automatic mode, a track at a new rate begins after a short gap, and its first instant can be resampled. Manual mode avoids both.

Will sounds from other apps play too?

Apple Music shares the output, so other apps' sounds mix in. Close other audio sources while you listen.

Which file types does it detect?

AIFF and WAV files are not detected yet.
